About me

I am a high school teacher in the Edmonton and St. Albert area with more than 20 years of classroom experience. Earlier in my career, I was honoured to be named a finalist for the Alberta Excellence in Teaching Award. Teaching remains the foundation of my work, but what and how I teach have continued to evolve.

Before moving into Communication Technologies, I spent many years teaching all levels of high school mathematics. That experience taught me how to break complex ideas into clear, manageable steps and how to recognize when students need a different way into a problem. I now teach Communication Technologies and Design, along with other Career and Technology Studies courses. My current classroom brings together digital design, photography, media production and emerging technologies.

I also run an in-school print shop where students gain experience with large-format printing, vinyl production, 3D printing and real client work. I am building a student broadcast program and studio that will connect hands-on media production with CTS credits.

My interest in broadcasting goes back to the beginning of my career. After earning a Radio and Television diploma from NAIT, I worked in broadcast radio from 1997 to 2002. I later completed my Bachelor of Education at the University of Alberta and moved into teaching. Radio taught me how to communicate clearly, work under pressure and connect with an audience. Those skills have stayed with me throughout my teaching career.

I am currently completing an MSc Artificial Intelligence through the University of Essex Online. My coursework has included statistics, R, machine learning and agent-based systems. My results to date are at the Distinction level, with feedback recognizing the distinction I draw between producing an output and judging whether it can be trusted.

That distinction is central to how I approach AI. Learning how these systems work matters, but so does questioning their purpose. Who sets the objectives? Whose interests do those objectives represent? Who benefits when a system works, and who bears the consequences when it does not?

In my own practice, I design AI-supported tools and workflows that reduce routine teacher workload while keeping professional judgment in charge. I build course and curriculum resources that make lessons clearer, more self-paced and easier for students to follow. I also develop reusable AI tools and prompts for planning, assessment support and study materials. At home, I experiment with local AI models to better understand the technology and explore what can be done outside large commercial platforms.

I treat AI as something to design around, not simply another tool to use. For me, a useful system should save time rather than add steps. It should make the work clearer without hiding important decisions. Most importantly, it should support the judgment of teachers and students rather than quietly replacing it.

Leadership and service have also been a long-standing part of my career. I currently serve as a School Representative and as a member of my local’s Teacher Welfare Committee. In these roles, I represent colleagues and take part in collective bargaining. Earlier in my career, I spent six years on the local executive. Together, these roles have given me two decades of experience in workplace organization, representation and communicating on behalf of colleagues.

I plan to remain connected to the classroom while widening the reach of what I have learned. I am interested in AI literacy, curriculum design and enablement work that helps teachers, schools and organizations adopt AI in practical, responsible and honest ways. I am open to opportunities beyond my own classroom, but teaching will remain my base.

This portfolio brings together my teaching, applied AI work, graduate studies and current projects. I invite you to explore the work and see how these areas connect.

Current interests

  • Responsible uses of artificial intelligence in education
  • Interpretable data analysis and defensible decision making
  • Accessible learning design
  • Reflective professional development
