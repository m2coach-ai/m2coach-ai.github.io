Module 1. Open the module page

Results: Collaborative Learning Discussion: Distinction; End of Module Assignment (Parts A and B): Distinction. Read the feedback

Launch into Computing: learning outcomes and the artefacts that evidence each
Learning outcomeHow the artefacts meet itEvidence
Identify and critically analyse computing challenges and processes in business systems, accounting for the current enterprise landscape.Together, these artefacts show that computing challenges emerge through the interaction of technology, people and organisational conditions because the education discussion examines what students may quietly lose when AI becomes the default, while the SafeCatch and Maplewood plans trace operational risk through human behaviour and business processes. They also show that resilience must be tested in practice because safeguards, recovery targets and legal compliance on paper mean little if an employee cannot report a mistake safely or a business cannot restore its essential services.
  • Unit 4 discussion: AI in education
  • Threat Assessment and Mitigation Plan for a Phishing Attack
  • Disaster Recovery Plan for a Cloud-Based E-Commerce Organisation
Gather and synthesise information from multiple sources (including internet resources and business publications) to aid in the systematic design and analysis of computing challenges.These artefacts show how I gather and weigh different forms of evidence because I tested academic arguments against legislation, NIST and ISO standards, AWS documentation, NCSC guidance, Verizon’s industry data and findings from Canadian privacy commissioners, judging each source by its authority, evidence and relevance to the computing challenge. Combining those sources supported more systematic analysis than any one source could provide because I could set industry evidence about how incidents occur against research explaining why they occur, then use standards, technical documentation and law to design practical controls, recovery processes and enforceable accountability.
  • AI: Balancing Innovation with Security and Ethical Considerations
  • Threat Assessment and Mitigation Plan for a Phishing Attack
  • Disaster Recovery Plan for a Cloud-Based E-Commerce Organisation
  • Unit 4 discussion: AI in education
Critically evaluate appropriate methodologies, tools and techniques to mitigate and/or solve computing issues and their business impact.These artefacts show that the right method depends on the failure a business needs to survive because Waterfall, Agile, Python, JavaScript and COBIT 2019 address different risks rather than offering one universally correct solution. The work also shows why tools must be judged through their outputs and consequences because code can run without error, tests can appear green and an AI system can remain operational while still producing unsafe or misleading results.
  • Comparative Analysis: Waterfall vs Agile
  • A1: Code Exploration
  • A2: Language Comparison Report
  • Developing a Resilient and Strategic IT Framework
Articulate the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues faced by computing professionals.Across these artefacts, professional responsibility continues after software begins working because apparently valid outputs can still reproduce bias, erase people from records or breach legal duties of accuracy and accountability. The cases also show that ethical principles require enforceable accountability because the Horizon, Amazon, COMPAS, Air Canada and PowerSchool examples reveal the consequences of trusting apparently credible systems without adequate professional scrutiny or operational preparation.
  • A3: Professional Reflection
  • AI: Balancing Innovation with Security and Ethical Considerations
  • Disaster Recovery Plan for a Cloud-Based E-Commerce Organisation

Understanding Artificial Intelligence

Module 2. Open the module page

Results: Collaborative Learning Discussion: Distinction; Research report (Unit 9): Distinction; AI Solution Implementation (Unit 12): Distinction. Read the feedback

Understanding Artificial Intelligence: learning outcomes and the artefacts that evidence each
Learning outcomeHow the artefacts meet itEvidence
Understand the legal, ethical and professional issues brought up by AI and the impact of AI on society.These artefacts show that AI’s social value cannot be separated from legal, ethical and professional responsibility because its deployment can conceal ghost work, impose environmental costs and leave no one clearly answerable when a system causes harm. They also show why human oversight must involve meaningful understanding because requiring a clinician to answer for a decision they cannot follow creates a moral crumple zone rather than genuine accountability.
  • Collaborative Learning Discussion 1
  • Collaborative Learning Discussion 2
  • The Smallest Defensible Model
Understand and critically analyse the essential concepts, principles, methods, techniques and problems of AI.Taken together, these artefacts show how modern AI developed because they connect the convergence of data, hardware and algorithms with the different architectures and methods used in machine learning, deep learning and large language models. They also show that model capability and opacity can rise together because The Smallest Defensible Model compares increasingly powerful systems while asking whether their decisions can still be understood, challenged and defended.
  • Collaborative Learning Discussion 1
  • Collaborative Learning Discussion 2
  • The Smallest Defensible Model
Demonstrate a critical understanding of data requirements and programming paradigms applicable to AI.The feasibility study shows how data requirements shape an AI project because it justifies using the Wisconsin breast cancer dataset and examines how its 569 cases and 30 features support the proposed classification task. It also demonstrates the importance of programming paradigms because it selects supervised learning over unsupervised learning and applies a shared evaluation process to logistic regression and Random Forest.
  • Instantiating the Smallest Defensible Model
Apply and evaluate critically the various methods, tools and technologies applied to an AI project in order to develop an effective plan and delivery of solutions to a business problem.The feasibility study shows how AI methods can be evaluated against a business problem because it gives a board evidence from accuracy, false negatives, false positives and explainability rather than asking it to choose a model on technical reputation alone. It also shows why the smallest defensible model can be the stronger business solution because both models achieved 96.5 percent accuracy, while Random Forest produced one more false negative and offered no gain sufficient to justify its additional complexity.
  • Instantiating the Smallest Defensible Model

Numerical Analysis: learning outcomes and the artefacts that evidence each
Learning outcomeHow the artefacts meet itEvidence
Systematic understanding of the key mathematical and statistical concepts and techniques which underpin mechanisms in Data Science and AI.The discussion post shows a strong association and a tiny p-value resting on an assumption that did not hold. The Unit 12 presentation shows an impressive R squared and an accuracy figure that borrowed their strength from how the data were collected and evaluated.
  • When statistical measures create false confidence
  • Earned or Borrowed?
  • From Output to Judgment
Apply mathematical and statistical methods in these fields to help in the decision-making process.The recovery-frequency entry and the annotated scripts apply frequency tables, tests and models in R. The Unit 12 presentation applies them to a decision: which numbers a screening clinic with a limited testing budget can trust.
  • Recovery reporting frequency
  • R scripts and plots
  • Earned or Borrowed?
Critically evaluate the use of statistical analysis and the numeric interpretation of results as aids in the decision-making process.Each item asks whether a result deserves trust: the failed independence behind a tiny p-value, the sample and the evaluation behind impressive model numbers, and the audit of the Brown table in the reflection and the figure and discussion built from it.
  • When statistical measures create false confidence
  • Earned or Borrowed?
  • From Output to Judgment
  • Brown Table II: the figure and the discussion
Critically appraise and present results of a statistical analysis to a diverse audience.The presentation is framed around a clinic question and not statistical jargon, and the reflection follows the What, So What, Now What structure, so both present the analysis to a listener and not only to a statistician.
  • Earned or Borrowed?
  • From Output to Judgment
