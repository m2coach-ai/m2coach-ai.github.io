Alice, Bob and the Black Friday TVs

About this activity

The task: Create an agent dialogue, using KQML and KIF, between two agents (named Alice and Bob).

Alice is an agent designed to procure stock and Bob is an agent that controls the stock levels for a warehouse. This dialogue should see Alice asking Bob about the available stock of 50 inch televisions, and also querying the number of HDMI slots the televisions have.

Alice, Procurement agent. Always in a hurry. Has to fill the Black Friday flyer with a 50 inch TV deal by Monday.

Bob, Warehouse stock agent. Knows every shelf and every count. Only says what he actually knows. Never guesses.

The brief: the flyer headline is "Plug in your console AND your soundbar." So Alice needs a 50 inch TV that is in stock and has enough HDMI ports. Only Bob can tell her.

The dialogue

Alice · ask-all · q1

Bob! What 50 inch TVs have you got, and how many of each?

(ask-all
  :sender     Alice
  :receiver   Bob
  :language   KIF
  :ontology   warehouse-stock
  :reply-with q1
  :content    (and (television ?p)
                   (screen-size ?p 50)
                   (stock-level ?p ?qty)))

Bob · tell · re q1

Two models. TV50-A: 120 on the shelf. TV50-B: 35.

(tell
  :sender      Bob
  :receiver    Alice
  :language    KIF
  :ontology    warehouse-stock
  :in-reply-to q1
  :content     (and
                 (television TV50-A)
                 (screen-size TV50-A 50)
                 (stock-level TV50-A 120)
                 (television TV50-B)
                 (screen-size TV50-B 50)
                 (stock-level TV50-B 35)))

Alice does the maths: 120 + 35 is plenty for the flyer. Now, the ports…

Alice · ask-one · q2

How many HDMI ports on TV50-A?

(ask-one
  :sender     Alice
  :receiver   Bob
  :language   KIF
  :ontology   warehouse-stock
  :reply-with q2
  :content    (hdmi-ports TV50-A ?n))

Bob · tell · re q2

Four.

(tell
  :sender      Bob
  :receiver    Alice
  :language    KIF
  :ontology    warehouse-stock
  :in-reply-to q2
  :content     (hdmi-ports TV50-A 4))

Console, soundbar and two to spare. TV50-A passes.

Alice · ask-one · q3

And TV50-B?

(ask-one
  :sender     Alice
  :receiver   Bob
  :language   KIF
  :ontology   warehouse-stock
  :reply-with q3
  :content    (hdmi-ports TV50-B ?n))

Bob · sorry · re q3

Got your question. No spec sheet on file for that one, so I won't guess.

(sorry
  :sender      Bob
  :receiver    Alice
  :language    KIF
  :ontology    warehouse-stock
  :in-reply-to q3
  :content     (hdmi-ports TV50-B ?n))

Alice puts TV50-A on the flyer. 120 units, four HDMI ports. Deal done.

Extension

Alice · ask-all · q4

(ask-all
  :sender     Alice
  :receiver   Bob
  :language   KIF
  :ontology   warehouse-stock
  :reply-with q4
  :content    (and (television ?p)
                   (screen-size ?p 50)
                   (hdmi-ports ?p ?n)))

How it works

The three layers of each message: KQML performative, KIF content and ontology
LayerJobHere
KQML performativeWhat kind of move is this?ask-all, ask-one, tell, sorry
KIF contentWhat exactly is being said?Logic sentences. Questions hold ?variables; answers are ground facts.
OntologyWhat do the words mean?warehouse-stock, shared by both agents

ask-all opens the conversation because Alice doesn't know how many 50 inch models exist. Once she wants one specific fact, she switches to ask-one. Each question carries a :reply-with label and each answer quotes it back in :in-reply-to, so answers match questions even when many conversations run at once. Bob's sorry means "understood, but I don't know", which is different from error, meaning "your message was broken".

The shared dictionary (ontology), in KIF

(forall (?p ?s)
  (=> (screen-size ?p ?s)
      (and (television ?p) (> ?s 0))))

(forall (?p ?q)
  (=> (stock-level ?p ?q)
      (and (integer ?q) (>= ?q 0))))

(forall (?p ?n)
  (=> (hdmi-ports ?p ?n)
      (and (television ?p) (integer ?n) (>= ?n 0))))

Screen size is in inches. That's a design choice, and the ontology is where it gets written down so both agents agree.

Learning outcomes for this activity

  • An understanding of the motivations for, and appropriate use of, agent-based computing.
  • An understanding of the main agent models in use today and their grounding in artificial intelligence research.

On the module page this is evidence for:

  • Apply and critically evaluate intelligent agent techniques to real-world problems, particularly where technical risk and uncertainty is involved. See it on the module page
