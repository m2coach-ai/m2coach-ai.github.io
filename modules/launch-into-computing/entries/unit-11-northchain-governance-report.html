Unit 11 / Governance

Developing a Resilient and Strategic IT Framework

A short report on NorthChain Logistics, a mid-sized AI-driven logistics provider: one risk, data poisoning, and how COBIT 2019 governs it.

The report

NorthChain Logistics is a mid-sized AI-driven logistics and fulfilment provider running warehouse automation, predictive routing, and shared data with retail clients. Its most significant cybersecurity risk is data poisoning. Because NorthChain’s routing and predictive maintenance systems depend on continuously learning models, an attacker who gradually corrupts training data could cause the AI to make subtly wrong decisions for months before anyone notices, eroding efficiency and customer trust (Bashynska and Prokopenko, 2024).

NorthChain’s strategic opportunity and its cybersecurity risk are the same capability. The routing model that saves two days of transit time is the same model a poisoning attack degrades invisibly. What separates advantage from attack surface is how the capability is governed (Bashynska and Prokopenko, 2024).

COBIT 2019 provides that governance. It aligns IT decisions to business strategy. Risk management sits in APO12, continuity in DSS04. Poisoning defences are not generic backup procedures. They are specific controls: validating and versioning training data, monitoring deployed models for behavioural drift, separating training environments from production, and retraining from verified baselines when drift is detected. Nguyen et al. (2025) find that logistics firms reduce digital transformation risk most effectively when technological controls, compliance, and organisational digital literacy move together. COBIT gives that movement a shape.

The ethical and professional implications follow. Lawful processing of customer and partner data under GDPR is the baseline, and the burden of proof rests with NorthChain. Bashynska and Prokopenko (2024) argue that public trust is itself a prerequisite for AI-driven business models. Customers and partners who discover after the fact that decisions affecting them were shaped by compromised data do not stay customers and partners for long.
