Launch into Computing

Learning outcomes

  1. Identify and critically analyse computing challenges and processes in business systems, accounting for the current enterprise landscape.

    Together, these artefacts show that computing challenges emerge through the interaction of technology, people and organisational conditions because the education discussion examines what students may quietly lose when AI becomes the default, while the SafeCatch and Maplewood plans trace operational risk through human behaviour and business processes. They also show that resilience must be tested in practice because safeguards, recovery targets and legal compliance on paper mean little if an employee cannot report a mistake safely or a business cannot restore its essential services.

    Evidence

    • Unit 4 discussion: AI in education
    • Threat Assessment and Mitigation Plan for a Phishing Attack
    • Disaster Recovery Plan for a Cloud-Based E-Commerce Organisation

  2. Gather and synthesise information from multiple sources (including internet resources and business publications) to aid in the systematic design and analysis of computing challenges.

    These artefacts show how I gather and weigh different forms of evidence because I tested academic arguments against legislation, NIST and ISO standards, AWS documentation, NCSC guidance, Verizon’s industry data and findings from Canadian privacy commissioners, judging each source by its authority, evidence and relevance to the computing challenge. Combining those sources supported more systematic analysis than any one source could provide because I could set industry evidence about how incidents occur against research explaining why they occur, then use standards, technical documentation and law to design practical controls, recovery processes and enforceable accountability.

    Evidence

    • AI: Balancing Innovation with Security and Ethical Considerations
    • Threat Assessment and Mitigation Plan for a Phishing Attack
    • Disaster Recovery Plan for a Cloud-Based E-Commerce Organisation
    • Unit 4 discussion: AI in education

  3. Critically evaluate appropriate methodologies, tools and techniques to mitigate and/or solve computing issues and their business impact.

    These artefacts show that the right method depends on the failure a business needs to survive because Waterfall, Agile, Python, JavaScript and COBIT 2019 address different risks rather than offering one universally correct solution. The work also shows why tools must be judged through their outputs and consequences because code can run without error, tests can appear green and an AI system can remain operational while still producing unsafe or misleading results.

    Evidence

    • Comparative Analysis: Waterfall vs Agile
    • A1: Code Exploration
    • A2: Language Comparison Report
    • Developing a Resilient and Strategic IT Framework

  4. Articulate the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues faced by computing professionals.

    Across these artefacts, professional responsibility continues after software begins working because apparently valid outputs can still reproduce bias, erase people from records or breach legal duties of accuracy and accountability. The cases also show that ethical principles require enforceable accountability because the Horizon, Amazon, COMPAS, Air Canada and PowerSchool examples reveal the consequences of trusting apparently credible systems without adequate professional scrutiny or operational preparation.

    Evidence

    • A3: Professional Reflection
    • AI: Balancing Innovation with Security and Ethical Considerations
    • Disaster Recovery Plan for a Cloud-Based E-Commerce Organisation
