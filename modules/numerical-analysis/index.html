Numerical Analysis

The row that did not add up.

The third module of the programme: statistical analysis in R, from a first frequency table to a diabetes capstone, and a reflection on deciding whether numerical output deserves to be trusted.

Learning outcomes

  1. Systematic understanding of the key mathematical and statistical concepts and techniques which underpin mechanisms in Data Science and AI.

    The discussion post shows a strong association and a tiny p-value resting on an assumption that did not hold. The Unit 12 presentation shows an impressive R squared and an accuracy figure that borrowed their strength from how the data were collected and evaluated.

    Evidence

    • When statistical measures create false confidence
    • Earned or Borrowed?
    • From Output to Judgment

  2. Apply mathematical and statistical methods in these fields to help in the decision-making process.

    The recovery-frequency entry and the annotated scripts apply frequency tables, tests and models in R. The Unit 12 presentation applies them to a decision: which numbers a screening clinic with a limited testing budget can trust.

    Evidence

    • Recovery reporting frequency
    • R scripts and plots
    • Earned or Borrowed?

  3. Critically evaluate the use of statistical analysis and the numeric interpretation of results as aids in the decision-making process.

    Each item asks whether a result deserves trust: the failed independence behind a tiny p-value, the sample and the evaluation behind impressive model numbers, and the audit of the Brown table in the reflection and the figure and discussion built from it.

    Evidence

    • When statistical measures create false confidence
    • Earned or Borrowed?
    • From Output to Judgment
    • Brown Table II: the figure and the discussion

  4. Critically appraise and present results of a statistical analysis to a diverse audience.

    The presentation is framed around a clinic question and not statistical jargon, and the reflection follows the What, So What, Now What structure, so both present the analysis to a listener and not only to a statistician.

    Evidence

    • Earned or Borrowed?
    • From Output to Judgment
