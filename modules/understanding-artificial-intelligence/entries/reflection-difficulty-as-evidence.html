Learning to use difficulty as evidence

Module 2 moved me from recognizing a technical problem to diagnosing why it occurred.

About this reflection

About this page: Written for this e-portfolio after the module. It was not part of a submitted assessment. It follows the What, So What, Now What structure, and each claim links to the artefact passage that supports it.

What

Module 2 moved me from recognizing a technical problem to diagnosing why it occurred. In Instantiating the Smallest Defensible Model, I attempted to determine whether Random Forest improves enough on an interpretable logistic regression baseline to justify its greater complexity. My first approach produced a misleading tie: both models reached 96.5 per cent accuracy. That exposed a weakness in my testing, because accuracy hid the distribution of errors, including the false negatives that matter most. The analysis of outputs shows where that figure stopped being enough.

Instead of removing the unsuccessful attempt, I used it to trace the problem. I compared the confusion matrices, ran an exact McNemar test on the paired errors, and checked per-fold recall against a dummy baseline. I identified that a one-case difference on 42 malignant test cases could not support a winner (p = 1.000), and I revised my conclusion to a narrower claim: “this test shows no advantage sufficient to justify escalation.”

So what

The important learning was not simply that the revised method worked. I began to see an error as information about my reasoning. It showed where I had made an assumption, skipped a check or relied on an explanation that was more confident than the evidence allowed. The threshold analysis in the same section showed one such assumption: “The operating point moves the costly error more than the choice between these two algorithms does.”

This changed the standard I applied to my own work. Completion answered, “Did I produce a result?” Evaluation asked, “What makes that result worth trusting?” The second question required evidence of testing, limitations and reproducibility. The transfer section sets out which validation methods this study did not run and why.

Now what

I will carry this diagnostic approach into the next module. For every major artefact, I will document at least one verification step, retain evidence of a meaningful correction and identify one limitation that the final result does not resolve. This will help me distinguish confidence earned through checking from confidence borrowed from a plausible-looking output.

Contribution statement

The experience, professional connection and final judgement are my own. Foody (2023) informed the argument that a single accuracy figure conceals class-level behaviour, and Dietterich (1998) informed the McNemar test of paired errors. Generative AI (Anthropic, 2026) assisted with brainstorming the project topic, guidance on the Jupyter environment, report structuring, the workflow diagram, and flagging syntax mistakes or logical oversights when prompted. The full use is set out in the AI-use declaration. I independently checked each suggestion, decided whether it was appropriate and revised the final work.
