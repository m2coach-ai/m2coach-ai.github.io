Module 2

Understanding Artificial Intelligence

The second module of the programme, from April 2026: machine learning, deep learning and large language models, governance and accountability, and a first applied classification study.

Learning outcomes

  1. Understand the legal, ethical and professional issues brought up by AI and the impact of AI on society.

    These artefacts show that AI’s social value cannot be separated from legal, ethical and professional responsibility because its deployment can conceal ghost work, impose environmental costs and leave no one clearly answerable when a system causes harm. They also show why human oversight must involve meaningful understanding because requiring a clinician to answer for a decision they cannot follow creates a moral crumple zone rather than genuine accountability.

    Evidence

    • Collaborative Learning Discussion 1
    • Collaborative Learning Discussion 2
    • The Smallest Defensible Model

  2. Understand and critically analyse the essential concepts, principles, methods, techniques and problems of AI.

    Taken together, these artefacts show how modern AI developed because they connect the convergence of data, hardware and algorithms with the different architectures and methods used in machine learning, deep learning and large language models. They also show that model capability and opacity can rise together because The Smallest Defensible Model compares increasingly powerful systems while asking whether their decisions can still be understood, challenged and defended.

    Evidence

    • Collaborative Learning Discussion 1
    • Collaborative Learning Discussion 2
    • The Smallest Defensible Model

  3. Demonstrate a critical understanding of data requirements and programming paradigms applicable to AI.

    The feasibility study shows how data requirements shape an AI project because it justifies using the Wisconsin breast cancer dataset and examines how its 569 cases and 30 features support the proposed classification task. It also demonstrates the importance of programming paradigms because it selects supervised learning over unsupervised learning and applies a shared evaluation process to logistic regression and Random Forest.

    Evidence

    • Instantiating the Smallest Defensible Model

  4. Apply and evaluate critically the various methods, tools and technologies applied to an AI project in order to develop an effective plan and delivery of solutions to a business problem.

    The feasibility study shows how AI methods can be evaluated against a business problem because it gives a board evidence from accuracy, false negatives, false positives and explainability rather than asking it to choose a model on technical reputation alone. It also shows why the smallest defensible model can be the stronger business solution because both models achieved 96.5 percent accuracy, while Random Forest produced one more false negative and offered no gain sufficient to justify its additional complexity.

    Evidence

    • Instantiating the Smallest Defensible Model
